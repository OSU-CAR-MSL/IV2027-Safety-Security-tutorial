About the tutorial

Trustworthy autonomous vehicles under uncertainty and adversarial threats

Autonomous vehicles depend on sensing, estimation, communication, and closed-loop control. Uncertain measurements, unexpected faults, and intentional cyber manipulation can each affect vehicle behavior. Drawing on the Ohio State graduate course ME/ECE 5555, Safety & Security of Autonomous Systems, this tutorial presents foundational methods and two independent research case studies, then relates their lessons to automotive safety and cybersecurity standards.

Understand safety

Explore safe sets, invariance, control barrier functions (CBFs), and risk-aware control under uncertain sensing.

Understand security

Study cyber-physical systems (CPS) threat models, measurement manipulation, residual-based detection, and their limitations.

Connect to practice

Apply the concepts to vehicle research demonstrations, industry standards, and an interactive assessment exercise.

Curriculum

Core topics and learning outcomes

Designed for graduate students, researchers, and automotive engineers from controls, robotics, intelligent transportation, AI, and cybersecurity.

Background: Basic linear algebra, probability, and feedback-control concepts. Prior knowledge of control barrier functions or automotive standards is not required.

Participants will learn to:

  • Distinguish sensing uncertainty, faults, and malicious manipulation in a vehicle feedback loop.
  • Interpret safe-set and control-barrier-function approaches to safety-critical control.
  • Recognize vehicle cyber-physical systems (CPS) attack surfaces and limitations of intrusion detection.
  • Relate safety and security analyses to relevant automotive standards.
  • Evaluate experimental evidence, assumptions, and open research challenges.
Case study A · safety

Risk-aware obstacle avoidance under sensing uncertainty

Multimodal camera, light detection and ranging (LiDAR), and vehicle-to-everything (V2X) sensing; Wasserstein-barycenter fusion; and conditional value at risk (CVaR)–control barrier function (CBF) safety filtering around a nominal model predictive controller (MPC), with full-scale vehicle and simulation evaluation.

Chang, Xu, Renganathan & Ahmed, Control Engineering Practice (2026).

Read the paper ↗
Case study B · security

Generative stealthy attacks on safety-critical vehicle CPS

Model-free adversarial measurement manipulation, stealth and impact objectives, intrusion-detection limitations, and vehicle simulations and hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) evaluations. Presented as a defensive threat-analysis lesson.

Mairaj ud din, Bhatti & Ahmed (2026).

Read the research paper ↗
Complementary, independent studies: the first examines safety under sensing uncertainty; the second evaluates intentional attacks and intrusion-detection limitations. The tutorial compares their assumptions and implications without claiming a jointly validated safety-security solution.

Assurance in practice

Automotive safety and cybersecurity standards

An accessible, practice-oriented guide to four complementary automotive standards, relating safety engineering, intended-function limitations, AI considerations, and cybersecurity risk assessment. The examples illustrate engineering relevance, not certification or compliance claims.

ISO 26262

Functional safety of road vehicles: hazards and risks associated with malfunctions of electrical and electronic systems.

ISO 21448 (SOTIF)

Safety of the Intended Functionality: potentially hazardous behavior from functional insufficiencies and foreseeable misuse.

ISO/PAS 8800

Road-vehicle safety and artificial intelligence: AI-specific considerations within vehicle safety engineering.

ISO/SAE 21434

Road-vehicle cybersecurity engineering: threat analysis, risk assessment, and cybersecurity practices.

Interactive example: What changes when unreliable positioning near a pedestrian crossing results from hardware malfunction, limitations of sensing, or adversarial manipulation?

Frequently asked questions

Do I need prior knowledge of safety-critical control or cybersecurity?

No. Basic undergraduate-level mathematics and feedback control will help, but specialized concepts are introduced in the tutorial.

Will this tutorial offer remote participation?

The tutorial is planned as an in-person event; live remote participation is not planned. The overview and references remain publicly available, and selected teaching materials may be posted after the event.
