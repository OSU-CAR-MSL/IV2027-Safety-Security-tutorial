About the tutorial

When vehicle intelligence meets physical safety

Autonomous vehicles depend on sensing, estimation, communication, and closed-loop control. Uncertain measurements, unexpected faults, and intentional cyber manipulation can all affect their behavior. This tutorial connects dynamical-systems foundations with cybersecurity concepts and modern automotive assurance practices.

01

Understand safety

Explore safe sets, invariance, control barrier functions, and risk-aware control under uncertain sensing.

02

Understand security

Study cyber-physical threat models, measurement manipulation, residual-based detection, and their limitations.

03

Connect to practice

Apply the concepts to vehicle research demonstrations, industry standards, and an interactive assessment exercise.

Curriculum

Core topics and learning outcomes

Designed for graduate students, researchers, and automotive engineers from controls, robotics, intelligent transportation, AI, and cybersecurity.

Background: Basic linear algebra, probability, and feedback-control concepts. Prior knowledge of control barrier functions or automotive standards is not required.

Participants will learn to:

  • Distinguish sensing uncertainty, faults, and malicious manipulation in a vehicle feedback loop.
  • Interpret safe-set and control-barrier-function approaches to safety-critical control.
  • Recognize vehicle CPS attack surfaces and limitations of intrusion detection.
  • Relate safety and security analyses to relevant automotive standards.
  • Evaluate experimental evidence, assumptions, and open research challenges.
Case study A · safety

Risk-aware obstacle avoidance under sensing uncertainty

Multi-modal camera, LiDAR, and V2X sensing; Wasserstein-barycenter fusion; and CVaR–CBF safety filtering around a nominal model-predictive controller, with vehicle and simulation evaluation.

Chang, Xu, Renganathan & Ahmed, Control Engineering Practice (2026).

Read the paper ↗
Case study B · security

Generative stealthy attacks on safety-critical vehicle CPS

Model-free adversarial measurement manipulation, stealth and impact objectives, intrusion-detection limitations, and vehicle/hardware-in-the-loop evaluations. Presented as a defensive threat-analysis lesson.

Mairaj ud din, Bhatti & Ahmed, arXiv preprint (2026).

Read the preprint ↗
Integrated perspective: One case study addresses safety under uncertainty; the other examines intentional manipulation of the feedback loop. They are complementary demonstrations, not a claim that one system has been experimentally defended against the other paper's attacks.

Assurance in practice

Automotive safety and cybersecurity standards

The standards module offers an orientation to four complementary engineering frameworks. It illustrates their roles without asserting that the featured research demonstrates certification or compliance.

ISO 26262

Functional safety of road vehicles: hazards and risks associated with malfunctions of electrical and electronic systems.

ISO 21448 (SOTIF)

Safety of the Intended Functionality: potentially hazardous behavior from functional insufficiencies and foreseeable misuse.

ISO/PAS 8800

Road-vehicle safety and artificial intelligence: AI-specific considerations within vehicle safety engineering.

ISO/SAE 21434

Road-vehicle cybersecurity engineering: threat analysis, risk assessment, and cybersecurity practices.

Interactive example: What changes when unreliable positioning near a pedestrian crossing results from hardware malfunction, limitations of sensing, or adversarial manipulation?

Frequently asked questions

Do I need prior knowledge of safety-critical control or cybersecurity?

No. Basic undergraduate-level mathematics and feedback control will help, but specialized concepts are introduced in the tutorial.

Will this tutorial offer remote participation?

The event is planned as in-person. Hybrid access will be offered only if IEEE IV 2027 permits it and suitable facilities are available. Publicly shareable materials can be posted afterward.

Registration and venue details will follow the IEEE IV 2027 conference's official instructions if this tutorial is accepted.
